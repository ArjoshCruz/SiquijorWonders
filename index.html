About Siquijor

Nestled in the heart of the Visayas, Siquijor is an island of mystery, beauty, and adventure. Known for its pristine beaches, cascading waterfalls, and rich folklore, Siquijor offers a unique blend of natural wonders and cultural heritage. Once called the “Island of Fire” due to the eerie glow of fireflies at night, Siquijor has long been associated with mysticism, faith healers, and age-old traditions. But beyond its mystical reputation, the island is home to stunning white sand beaches, enchanting caves, historic churches, and lush mountain landscapes. Whether you’re seeking relaxation by the turquoise waters, a thrilling island-hopping experience, or a deeper connection to its legendary past, Siquijor is a destination that promises a one-of-a-kind journey.

Travel Tips

Planning a trip to Siquijor? Here are some essential tips to help you make the most of your visit! From the best time to go and how to get around to must-know local customs, these travel tips will ensure a smooth and unforgettable island experience. Whether you're here for the beaches, waterfalls, or mystical charm, a little preparation goes a long way!

1. How to Get There

Siquijor is accessible by ferry from major ports like Dumaguete, Cebu, and Bohol. The most common and convenient entry point is through Dumaguete Port, with ferry rides taking about 1 hour to reach Siquijor. If you're coming from Cebu or Bohol, expect longer travel times, usually around 4–5 hours, depending on the ferry schedule and weather conditions.

Tip: Book your ferry tickets in advance, especially during peak seasons, as seats can fill up quickly. If you’re prone to seasickness, consider bringing motion sickness medication, as the waters can sometimes be rough.

2. Best Time to Visit

The ideal time to visit Siquijor is during the dry season (November to May) when the weather is sunny, and the seas are calm—perfect for island hopping, beach trips, and exploring waterfalls. If you’re interested in experiencing Siquijor’s mystical traditions, consider visiting during Holy Week, when the island's famous faith healers gather to perform traditional healing rituals. However, this is also a peak tourist season, so expect higher accommodation rates and larger crowds.

Tip: If you prefer a quieter visit with fewer tourists, June to October can still be a good time, though some rainy days may limit outdoor activities.

3. Getting Around

  • Motorbike Rental (₱300-₱500/day) - The most popular way to explore Siquijor at your own pace. The roads are generally in good condition, and traffic is light, making it a fun and easy ride. Make sure to wear a helmet and drive cautiously, especially on winding roads.
  • Tricycle or Habal-Habal (Motorcycle Taxi) – Ideal for small groups or solo travelers who prefer a guided trip. Prices vary depending on the distance and negotiation skills, but expect to pay around ₱1,000–₱1,500 for a whole-day tour.
  • Tour Packages – Many locals offer private or group day tours that cover must-visit spots, typically including Cambugahay Falls, Salagdoong Beach, and the Balete Tree. These are great if you want a hassle-free experience.

Tip: If you plan to rent a motorbike, check the brakes and fuel level before heading out. There are limited gas stations, so always refuel when you can!

4. Safety & Health Tips

  • Bring Cash – There are only a few ATMs on the island (mostly in Larena and Siquijor town), and some establishments don’t accept credit cards. Withdraw cash before arriving.
  • Wear Sunscreen & Bug Repellent – The sun can be intense, and mosquitoes are common, especially in forested areas. Protect your skin with reef-safe sunscreen and insect repellent.
  • Respect Local Beliefs – Siquijor is famous for its mystical traditions and faith healers. While exploring, be open-minded and respectful when locals talk about folklore, healing practices, or spiritual beliefs.
  • Check Ferry Schedules – Weather conditions can cause last-minute cancellations. If you have a flight after your trip, consider staying an extra day in Dumaguete to avoid missing it due to ferry delays.

Tip: Buy a local SIM card (Globe or Smart) for better connectivity, as Wi-Fi can be slow in some areas.
